How to actually use this

Do not read it front to back. Pick the one family you lose deals to most, open five objections, and say the scripts out loud. Reading them silently produces recognition, not recall, and recognition is useless at two in the afternoon when a buyer catches you off guard. Then rewrite them in your own words with your product in them. Five a day for a month covers the whole library and permanently changes how you sound under pressure.

The reframe matters more than the script. The script is one expression of the thinking; the reframe is the thinking. Learn the reframe and you can improvise a response to an objection that isn't in here at all, which is the actual goal. There are hundreds of objections and only about twelve underlying reasons for all of them.

Say this

Power move

Never defend the price. Investigate the comparison. 'Compared to what?' is the highest-yield four words in selling.

The five-step method behind every response

Every card in this library follows the same structure, because structure is what lets a nervous person perform under pressure. If you learn nothing else here, learn this sequence.

  • Pause. Two full seconds of silence. Instant responses sound rehearsed and tell the buyer they aren't being listened to.
  • Acknowledge. Reflect the concern back in your own words and let them confirm it. Now you're defining the problem together instead of arguing about it.
  • Isolate. "If we solved that, is there anything else?" This converts an infinite objection queue into a finite list.
  • Reframe. Change the axis of comparison. This is the part written at the top of every card.
  • Advance. End with a small forward-moving question, not a request for the whole decision.

Notice what's missing: arguing. When you answer a stated objection with an argument, you win the argument and lose the sale. When you answer it with a question, you find the real objection underneath, and the real one is almost always easier to solve than the one they said out loud.
